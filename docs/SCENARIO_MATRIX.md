## Notes on surprising results

Read with one caveat first: the matrix judges every candidate on **its own history** (alignment "each"). The alternatives (DBMF 2019, KMLM 2020, RSST/RSSB 2023) are therefore judged on short windows dominated by 2022, which flatters managed futures. The confidence column in `examples/scenario_matrix.xlsx` carries part of this: KMLM drops to Moderate or Low and RSST/RSSB to Low (short history, few stress windows covered), but DBMF keeps High because its history covers 5 of the 9 windows, including COVID and 2022. Scores do not adjust for history length.

1. **Managed futures and BTAL top most risk objectives in every profile.** Their histories contain 2022, the one year in which stocks and bonds fell together, and in which DBMF returned +44% and KMLM +63% (CAD). Scores saturate at +1.00 for several objectives; the unclipped score in the ranking separates them. Treat these as "strong on the evidence available, not proven over a full cycle".
2. **100% Bonds: adding equities is "Add" for reduce drawdown, faster recovery and risk-adjusted return** (VTV +0.92 on drawdown; SPY, RSP, USMV +1.00 on recovery). Expected once you look: the bonds-only profile's worst drawdown is 2022-23 and it is still under water, so a 10% equity sleeve that recovered quickly shortens the time under water and improves Sharpe.
3. **Currency and home-bias diversification ranks US Treasuries (AGG, IEF, SHY) above US equities.** Unhedged US bonds held in CAD have low correlation to Canadian equity and full USD exposure, which is what the objective measures. Correct by its definition; if the intent is equity home bias only, restrict the candidate list to equity sleeves.
4. **Income generation is mostly "Consider" with few "Add"**, even for ZWC.TO (score +0.95). The default guardrail caps Add when the extra yield costs over 0.5% a year of total return, and many high-yield candidates do (option-writing ZWC.TO is flagged; HYG and LQD give up return against a 60/40). Working as designed: yield alone does not earn an Add.
5. **RSST ranks near the top for inflation protection in the bond-heavy profiles** on about three years of data with few high-inflation months (Canadian CPI above 3% only in early 2023 within its history). Confidence is Low; do not rely on it.
6. **Risk of ruin: the bottom candidates score about 0.00**, not negative. In profiles where the 10-year probability of a 20% or 30% drawdown is already near zero (Conservative to 100% Bonds, or a short calm history), no candidate can lower it, so the change is 0.
7. **Rising-rate protection: every bond ETF is Do not add, TLT last in every profile.** Expected: in months when the 10-year yield rose, duration lost money; only managed futures (and partly RSST) gained.
8. **Verdicts change with the objective for 91% of (profile, candidate) pairs, and with the profile for 59% of (objective, candidate) pairs**, which is the intended behaviour: the same ETF solves some problems and not others, and a fix for a 100% Equities client is not always one for an Income client.
